# HAF — Hani Aviation Framework™

### An Open Methodology for Aviation Intelligence

HAF (Hani Aviation Framework™) is an open methodology designed to support the systematic research, analysis, validation, and communication of civil aviation intelligence.

It provides a structured approach for transforming aviation information from multiple sources into reliable, traceable, decision-useful intelligence.

The framework is designed for aviation professionals, researchers, safety and quality specialists, regulators, analysts, educators, and contributors working at the intersection of aviation and artificial intelligence.

---

## Why HAF?

Civil aviation produces an enormous amount of information every day:

* Regulatory publications
* ICAO and IATA materials
* Safety information
* Accident and incident reports
* Airline developments
* Airport operations
* Aircraft manufacturer announcements
* Technology developments
* Industry trends
* Financial and operational information
* Academic and technical research

The challenge is not simply finding information.

The challenge is determining:

**What matters?
What is confirmed?
What does it mean?
What could happen next?
And how should aviation professionals act on it?**

HAF was created to provide a repeatable methodology for answering those questions.

---

## Core Objective

HAF aims to help users produce aviation intelligence that is:

* Structured
* Evidence-based
* Traceable
* Contextual
* Professionally written
* Operationally relevant
* Transparent about uncertainty
* Suitable for human decision-making

The framework does not replace professional judgment.

It is designed to strengthen it.

---

## Core Methodology

HAF follows a structured intelligence workflow:

### 1. Identify

Identify the aviation development, question, trend, risk, or information requirement.

### 2. Collect

Collect relevant information from authoritative and reliable sources.

### 3. Verify

Separate confirmed information from assumptions, interpretation, speculation, and unsupported claims.

### 4. Contextualize

Place the information within its regulatory, operational, technological, economic, and safety context.

### 5. Analyze

Determine the significance, implications, relationships, risks, opportunities, and potential developments.

### 6. Synthesize

Convert the research into concise and decision-useful aviation intelligence.

### 7. Communicate

Present the findings in a format appropriate for the intended audience.

### 8. Review

Apply a structured editorial and quality review before publication.

## AI-Assisted Aviation Intelligence

HAF is designed to support responsible use of artificial intelligence in aviation research and analysis.

AI may assist with:

* Research structuring
* Information classification
* Comparative analysis
* Drafting
* Editorial review
* Pattern identification
* Research workflows
* Knowledge organization

However, AI-generated information should not automatically be treated as fact.

Human verification remains essential, particularly for:

* Safety information
* Regulatory requirements
* Operational decisions
* Legal matters
* Compliance
* Accident and incident analysis
* Security-related information

---

## Source Hierarchy

HAF encourages users to prioritize authoritative sources.

Examples include:

1. ICAO
2. National aviation authorities
3. EASA
4. FAA
5. IATA
6. Airlines and airports
7. Aircraft manufacturers
8. Official investigation authorities
9. Peer-reviewed research
10. Reputable industry publications

Media reports and secondary sources may provide useful leads, but important claims should be independently verified whenever possible.

---

## Editorial Ethics

HAF includes an editorial ethics component intended to promote:

* Accuracy
* Source transparency
* Attribution
* Separation of fact and interpretation
* Responsible use of AI
* Avoidance of fabricated information
* Appropriate handling of uncertainty
* Respect for intellectual property
* Correction of identified errors

The framework is particularly concerned with preventing the presentation of AI-generated assumptions as established aviation facts.
